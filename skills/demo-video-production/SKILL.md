# Demo Video Production

Create accurate, readable demonstrations of real product behavior. Resolve the
product, audience, environment, capture surface, brand assets, voice, budget and
delivery format from the request. No personal voice, browser, monitor, project,
licensed service plan or previously approved audition is assumed.

## Prepare the story

- Verify the environment, identity, tenant and source material before filming.
- Use synthetic names and data. Remove unrelated accounts, inboxes and windows.
- Script scenes with narration, exact actions, expected results and approximate
  duration. Explain purpose rather than narrating every click.
- Rehearse the real workflow through the final output, export and reload. A mock,
  dry run, health response or assistant success message is insufficient proof.
- Inspect generated documents for facts, page layout, source links and actual
  brand images. Check the download path if the story promises downloads.
- For automation demos, verify real triggers, timezone, duplicate suppression,
  matching/nonmatching cases and end conditions. Keep rehearsal triggers paused
  outside the authorized test window. Preserve drafts unsent unless sending is
  explicitly authorized.
- Fix product blockers only within current scope; recording does not authorize
  broad product repair, deployment, data writes or external communication.

## Capture and edit

Read [capture-and-edit.md](references/capture-and-edit.md) when selecting and
validating capture/render tools. Use an existing project editor when available.

- Use the user-approved browser/profile and exact capture surface. Verify a
  short test clip before a long take; ensure unrelated content is not captured.
- Prefer moving footage of actual actions and results. Do not reconstruct UI
  to disguise failed behavior or narrate old footage as a newly performed action.
- Keep bounded scenes, scene/version identifiers and private original recordings.
- Add the current project's brand assets, captions and restrained annotations
  outside important UI. Test readability at the intended viewing size.
- Remove dead space and errors. Label shortened processing time and retain
  enough result time for viewers to inspect it. Avoid misleading freeze padding.
- Reuse unchanged scenes, narration and assets when a revision is narrow.

## Narration and voice

Use the requested tone and an explicitly selected, available voice. A cloned
voice requires permission from its owner; possession of an account is not that
permission. Verify service access, licensing, usage allowance and spending
before paid generation. Do not infer a voice ID from a display name.

Audition a short representative excerpt before a full take. Record settings and
feedback in the private project. Approval applies to that project and sample,
not unrelated future work. Keep delivery natural, pronounce product terms
correctly and avoid invented personal anecdotes or endorsements.

Generate scene by scene. Keep script edits distinct from delivery packages until
audio, captions and timing are updated. Derive captions from final audio and
re-time after speed changes. Transcript agreement does not replace listening.

## Verification and delivery

Review one complete demo before continuing a series. Check factual accuracy,
clean framing, readability, pronunciation, timing and agreement among the UI,
narration, captions and annotations. A successful render is not style approval.

Verify resolution, frame rate, audio tracks, duration, full decode and transition
frames. Report any unavailable listening or visual review explicitly. Package
the review video, captions, script, shot list, editable source/media and concise
verification record. Share only clean cuts; retain noisy source captures privately.

Publication, upload, delivery messages and deployment require current task
authority. Preserve review drafts until delivery is requested. Save only reusable
lessons to this skill; keep personal preferences, account state, voice selections,
review notes and resource identifiers in private project configuration.
